# Editorial rubrics

Run separate passes. A single “improve this” prompt encourages confident mush.
Every continuity finding cites both relevant passages, IDs, or obligations.

## Causality and timeline

- Are effects supported by causes visible or intentionally concealed?
- Are duration, simultaneity, travel, deadlines, and event order possible?
- Did an open element disappear without resolution or deliberate abandonment?
- Does the scene alter meaningful state, or can it be removed unchanged?

## Character integrity

- What does the character want here, and what do they know?
- Is the action available given skills, resources, injuries, and location?
- Does it follow from values, fears, relationships, or established contradiction?
- If behavior changes, what pressure or transformation earns it?
- Is the plot advancing because of the person, or despite them?

Do not demand consistency so rigidly that people become deterministic. Surprise
is good; arbitrary convenience is not.

## World and factual consistency

- Do technology, magic, institutions, geography, and social norms obey their
  established constraints and costs?
- Are names, ages, appearances, quantities, possessions, injuries, and locations
  stable or explicitly changed?
- Is a character treating a rumor or belief as objective canon without cause?

## Knowledge and obligation

- Who knows, suspects, misbelieves, or conceals each fact?
- Did anyone act on information they could not possess?
- Which setups, clues, promises, debts, and reader questions opened or advanced?
- Are required payoffs resolved by the promised ending?
- Does a child version re-establish what a new reader needs?

## POV, tone, and prose

- Is narrative distance stable within the intended unit?
- Does language belong to the narrator/POV rather than a generic house voice?
- Does dialogue encode status, motive, evasion, and relationship rather than
  merely transferring facts?
- Is emotional subtext dramatized before it is named?
- Are descriptions selective and consequential?

## Pacing and necessity

- What changes in this beat or scene?
- Does escalation arise from choices and consequences rather than random events?
- Has long-form planning produced repetitive scene functions?
- Would compression strengthen the work?

## AI-tell and freshness pass

Flag clichés, redundant exposition, generic emotional summaries, purple prose,
forced metaphors, repetitive sentence rhythm, over-explained themes, interchangeable
dialogue, repeated plot devices, and tidy moral conclusions the story did not earn.

Compare against `style/novelty-ledger.md`. Repetition may be motif; if so, state
its purpose. Otherwise alter the mechanism, not merely its nouns.

## Finding format

```text
Severity: blocker | concern | suggestion
Lens: <rubric>
Evidence A: <file/scene/quote or fact ID>
Evidence B: <conflicting file/scene/quote or obligation>
Why it matters: <reader-visible consequence>
Smallest repair: <local option>
Structural alternative: <only when local repair is dishonest>
Confidence: high | medium | low
```

An LLM review is evidence, not authority. Human review decides interest,
emotional truth, humor, ambiguity, and whether a technically consistent story is
worth reading.
