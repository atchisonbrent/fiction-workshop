# Drafting for quality before the final pass

Load before drafting a new story or continuing an arc. This is the default
workflow, scaled to the brief, not a prescribed plot formula. It supplements
[scope planning](scope-and-expansion.md), [voice](influence-and-voice.md) and
[editorial review](editorial-rubrics.md); those remain the owners of their rules.

Use the [default lens selection](editorial-rubrics.md#default-lens-selection)
to separate writer preparation from editor diagnosis. Check
[emotional credibility](editorial-rubrics.md#emotional-credibility-and-relationships)
at chapter and arc boundaries without forcing every feeling to resolve.

## 1. Establish a destination, not a script

Before long-form prose, record the reader promise, central character question,
provisional consequential ending and aftermath in the existing kernel/outline.
Connect the next arc to that destination through choices and consequences.
Outline its important turns and dependencies, not every line of conversation.
For a short work, a compact causal/emotional sequence is sufficient; do not
manufacture arcs or chapters. Respect explicit discovery-writing preferences.

Plan near-term scenes more closely than distant ones. Include what the reader
will experience, not only which information must be delivered. Leave room for
pleasure, uncertainty, surprising responses and an ending better than the plan.
When discovery changes the route, reconcile the outline and affected setups;
never force a character to make an implausible choice to preserve an outline.
A promising tangent can be saved outside canon rather than expanding the brief.

## 2. Calibrate voices before multiplying scenes

Use the character-voice preparation in influence-and-voice.md. Try the principal
voices in a short exchange with different wants and a concrete activity, then in
a contrasting emotional or social context. Prefer an actual opening scene when
it tests the necessary range. Otherwise keep exploratory material explicitly
non-canon; do not insert a disposable exercise as filler in the manuscript.

Read the exchange as speech, not a list of positions. Could these people trade
lines unchanged? Does each pursue something in a different way? Can competence
coexist with awkwardness or bad judgment? Fix a shared authorial cadence here
before it propagates. Retain a few original, accepted voice anchors in the
existing style/character notes, with source locations when from the manuscript.
They are references, not catchphrases to repeat. Reader calibration is optional
unless requested; an autonomous full-story commission must not stall waiting
for chapter-by-chapter approval.

## 3. Draft a scene, check a chapter, keep moving

Before a scene, retrieve relevant source prose and note only useful essentials:
who is present, immediate wants, knowledge limits, physical activity/location,
intended reader experience, and the change or deliberate unresolved exit. Check
the most similar earlier conversation: what is different now? Use existing
outline/review notes; no new mandatory schema or form per utterance.

Draft the full scene rather than a summary of its important interaction. Then
read consecutive turns with action beats and transitions. Check:

- speech belongs to this person, listener and situation;
- replies connect to what was actually said, including after local rewrites;
- consequential action is physically and emotionally followable;
- discovery, reaction and aftermath have enough room, without compulsory expansion;
- narration does not explain a meaning already carried by behavior;
- the conversation changes something or offers a worthwhile experience rather
  than restating a settled lesson with new nouns.

At the chapter boundary, read the chapter continuously with its entrance from
the predecessor and its intended exit. Correct clear voice, causality, missing
setup and comprehension defects before drafting dependent scenes. Record a
structural uncertainty rather than polishing around it. Continue when the
chapter is coherent and its known dependencies are supported; it need not be
publication-perfect. No fixed chapter length, revision count, or cliffhanger
requirement. A chapter may contain several scenes or part of a larger sequence.

Update accepted canon, knowledge, promises and the next-scene plan. Do not
promote an exploratory passage or proposed fix into accepted state.

## 4. Check development across the arc

At meaningful movement boundaries, compare the actual draft to the outline.
Trace recurring conversations by participants, purpose, new knowledge, changed
relationship and outcome. If two exchanges perform the same work, consider
cutting, combining, summarizing, or changing their function. Different vocabulary
alone is not a repair. Preserve deliberate callbacks and unresolved concerns
that develop; do not erase character memory to make an exchange look new.

Read the arc continuously for pacing and cumulative emotional movement. If a
voice defect is systemic, inspect that character's earlier and later scenes,
including quieter appearances, rather than patching the reported example alone.
Repair structure before expensive final line polish. An arc checkpoint does not
replace whole-work convergence and final reading in scope-and-expansion.md.

## 5. Finish with evidence, not perpetual polishing

Use the existing editorial-review and release procedures. Independent review is
an additional perspective at an agreed useful boundary, not mandatory after
every chapter and not a substitute for author reading. After repairs, reread
changed exchanges with surrounding prose and affected dependencies. A whole-book
pass remains necessary for cumulative pacing, promises and character development;
local checks cannot certify these in isolation.

Stop when required findings are repaired or explicitly adjudicated, the intended
whole-work experience survives continuous reading, and delivery checks pass.
Do not launch another generic polish merely because further changes are possible.
Record recurring feedback in its existing craft owner, not a growing list of
story-specific prohibitions. Judge this workflow on subsequent writing: where
readers still stumble and which repairs recur. No measured improvement or reduced
revision count is claimed until later use supplies that evidence.

## Sources and adaptation

Practitioner guidance retrieved 2026-09-08, not controlled evidence of an optimal
AI writing method:

- [Writing Excuses 12.22: Hybrid Outlining and Discovery Writing](https://writingexcuses.com/12-22-hybrid-outlining-and-discovery-writing/): combine directional planning with character discovery; revise or discard attractive detours.
- [Writing Excuses 13.13: Character Voice](https://writingexcuses.com/13-13-character-voice/): background, syntax, vocabulary and intention differentiate voices; intelligence is not a universal formal register.
- [Writing Excuses 18.28: Conversational Dialogue](https://writingexcuses.com/18-28-writing-conversational-dialogue/): distinguish author purpose from speaker purpose; naturalistic dialogue is selective, contextual and partly nonverbal.
- [Writing Excuses 12.20: Retrofitting Structure](https://writingexcuses.com/12-20-retrofitting-structure-into-a-first-draft/): inspect promises and scene functions across the work; successful local pieces can form an unsatisfying whole.
- [Monica Cox: Why Your Revision Shouldn't Start on Page One](https://janefriedman.com/why-your-revision-shouldnt-start-on-page-one/): diagnose global problems before line editing. The local checks above catch propagating defects, not final polish before structure settles.

The staged cadence and acceptance boundaries are this workshop's synthesis.
Exercises from sources are optional diagnostics, not universal deletion ratios,
required speech quirks, or rules that every scene must fail or contain conflict.
