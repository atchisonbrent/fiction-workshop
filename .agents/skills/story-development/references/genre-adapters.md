# Genre adapters

Apply only adapters the story earns. They supplement the core model and do not
force three acts, a hero’s journey, or any other universal skeleton.

## Mystery

Track clue provenance, discoverability, who has encountered each clue, suspect
knowledge, false leads, withheld evidence, and the fair-play contract. The
solution must follow from available evidence even if readers miss it.

## Thriller

Track deadlines, travel, communications, resources, operational geography,
security assumptions, and escalation. Competent opposition should react to
changed conditions rather than wait politely for the protagonist.

## Romance

Track attraction, trust, vulnerability, consent, boundaries, relationship state,
and internal/external obstacles. Conflict should arise from values and risk, not
an avoidable misunderstanding kept alive by authorial force.

## Horror

Track threat rules, exposure, contamination or knowledge state, safe/unsafe
assumptions, and the dread curve. Decide what remains unknowable. Consistency of
threat does not require complete explanation.

## Science fiction

Track technological capability, limits, costs, scale, institutions, logistics,
and social consequences. Technology is part of culture and power, not a box of
miracles opened when the plot becomes inconvenient.

## Fantasy

Track capability source, cost, constraints, institutions, geography, inheritance,
ritual, and who understands the rules. Preserve mystery by distinguishing true
rules from character theories.

## Comedy

Track status, setup/payoff, reversals, running mechanisms, and whose dignity bears
the cost. Prefer humor arising from character, institution, scale mismatch, or
consequence over interchangeable quips.

## Literary and experimental fiction

Track thematic pressure, image systems, formal constraints, interior change,
and intentional ambiguity. Do not “resolve” ambiguity merely because the canon
model can represent an answer.

## Crossover fiction

Maintain separate source-universe canon and voice notes. Record which continuity
version is in use. Characters from different settings should differ in assumptions
about authority, scarcity, violence, personhood, religion, technology, and what
counts as normal—not only vocabulary.

Treat source material as reference, not text to reproduce. Private fan work still
benefits from clear attribution and a publication/rights review before any public
or commercial use.
